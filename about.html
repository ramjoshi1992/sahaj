About

sahaj plays one continuous piece of music, put together while you listen, for the hour you opened it and the weather where you are.

The name

Sahaj is Sanskrit for effortless — natural, unforced, the way something is when it isn't being made to happen. The word comes from saha, together, and ja, born: born along with you, rather than acquired.

That is the whole intention. You say one thing — what you want — and everything else is worked out for you. There is nothing to configure and nothing to choose from. It should take no effort at all.

What it does

You say what you want — to settle, to sleep, to shut out the noise — and how long you have. Everything after that is worked out for you.

The time of day and the weather decide which version of that mood you get. Morning under a clear sky gives you something brighter than the same mood at nine in the evening, or in the rain. Underneath the music there's usually a quiet layer of somewhere — rain outside, birds, a fire — chosen to suit where you are, and you can change it or turn it off at any point.

The background is the real sky. Not a picture of one: the colours are worked out from where the sun actually is above you, so a session that starts at sunset genuinely darkens while it plays. On a clear night the stars are the ones that are really up there, in the positions they're really in.

How the music was made

Every mood began with a written description — the instruments, the feel, how the energy should rise and fall — and three of those descriptions per mood, deliberately different from each other, were turned into music by an AI model. Those three pieces became the seeds.

Each seed was then used to grow a small family of related pieces: variations that share its key, its tempo and its character without being the same music. Twelve pieces per mood, per time of day. A little over two hundred and fifty altogether.

A session is built from those families. It opens with a seed, moves through that family's variations, and closes with the seed again — then does the same with the next family. Because everything inside a family is closely related, you never hear a join. The only real changes are the two or three moments where one family hands over to another, and those are given a breath of near-silence so they feel like a section ending rather than a seam.

The order is different every time. Which family opens, which closes, which variation follows which, where each one is entered — all of it is decided fresh. Two sessions of the same mood, the same length, on the same evening won't be the same piece.

Why it isn't a playlist

Nothing here exists as a finished track. There's no file to skip to and no recording to find somewhere else. The music is assembled in your browser as it plays, which is why it can be exactly as long as you asked for and end properly rather than fading out mid-phrase.

What it doesn't do

No advertising. Nothing is sold to anyone. If you use it without an account — which is the default — nothing about you leaves your device at all. If you make one, it stores what you listened to and how it landed, and nothing else: no location, no identifying details.
